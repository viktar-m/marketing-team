# X (Twitter)

Platform specs change. Treat the numbers below as working defaults and check the platform's current guidance when precision matters.

## Audience and tone
Fast, conversational, opinionated. Rewards clarity, wit and specificity; short sentences; no corporate voice. Replies and quote posts matter as much as original posts.

## Length and layout
- Standard posts: 280 characters (longer posts exist for subscribers, but assume 280 unless the brief says the account has longer posts). Count characters, including spaces, links and hashtags. A link counts as 23 characters.
- The best posts are often 70–150 characters — one sharp idea.
- Line breaks are fine; keep it scannable.

## Formats
- **Single post:** one tip, take, question or announcement.
- **Thread:** for a list, story or how-to. 4–8 posts. Post 1 is the hook and promises the payoff ("How we cut X in half — 5 steps 🧵" if emojis suit the voice). Each post must stand alone. Number them (1/6) if the brief's style allows. Last post: summary + CTA + link.
- **Image post:** 16:9 (1600×900) or square; text in the image short and large.
- **Poll:** 2–4 options, useful for engagement and audience research.

## Links
Put links at the end of a single post, or in the last post of a thread. For reach-focused posts, consider posting the link as a reply.

## Hashtags and mentions
- 0–2 hashtags. More looks like spam.
- Tag accounts only when relevant (a partner, a source you're citing).

## CTA patterns
A question that's easy to answer in one line, "repost if…" sparingly, or a single link.
