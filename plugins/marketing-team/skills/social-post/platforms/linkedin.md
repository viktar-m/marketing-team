# LinkedIn

Platform specs change. Treat the numbers below as working defaults and check the platform's current guidance when precision matters.

## Audience and tone
Professionals reading at work or on a commute. Rewards insight, experience and usefulness; punishes obvious selling. Write as a knowledgeable person, not a press release — first person plural ("we") for company pages, first person singular for posts written for an individual's profile.

## Length and layout
- Hard limit about 3,000 characters. Sweet spot: 600–1,300 characters for text posts.
- Only the first two or three lines show before "…see more" (roughly the first 150–200 characters). The hook must land there.
- One idea per line or short paragraph; blank lines between them. Avoid walls of text.
- Lists with simple symbols or numbers work well. Use emojis sparingly, if the brand voice allows them at all.

## Formats
- **Text post:** story, lesson, opinion, how-to. The default.
- **Image post:** a single strong image (1200×1200 or 1080×1350 portrait) with a short post.
- **Carousel / document post:** 5–10 slides of a framework, checklist or step-by-step. Slide 1 is the hook, the last slide is the CTA. Provide slide-by-slide copy (headline + up to ~25 words per slide).
- **Short video:** under 90 seconds, captions on, hook in the first 3 seconds.

## Links
Posts with external links are widely reported to get less reach. Default: put the link in the first comment and say "link in the comments" in the post — unless traffic is the goal of this post, in which case keep the link in the post.

## Hashtags and mentions
- 3–5 relevant hashtags at the end, brand hashtags first.
- Mention (@) partners or people only when they're genuinely involved.

## CTA patterns
Ask a specific question that invites experience ("What's the one thing you'd add?"), invite saves for reference content, or point to the link in the comments.
