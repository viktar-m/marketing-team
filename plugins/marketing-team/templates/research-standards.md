# Research standards

Analysis is only as good as its facts. Generic output usually means thin or stale research. Every agent that researches a product, market or competitor follows these rules.

## 1. Read the client's own material completely

- Fetch **every** page of the client's site that bears on the question, not just the home page: product, pricing (all tiers, limits, minimums, discounts, trial terms), use cases, comparison and switching pages, docs and quick start, changelog, roadmap, security/quality pages, partner or referral programs, FAQ, terms, about. Follow links from the nav and footer.
- Write down the client's **own numbers** (prices, limits, discounts, referral rates, minimum seats, nonprofit pricing, SLAs) — they feed the calculations later.
- Look for the **decisions the client is facing**: open questions, "TBD", roadmap items, draft notes, placeholders. These often tell you what the analysis must answer.
- Note launch-readiness problems (placeholders, broken links, unverifiable claims) — they're findings.

## 2. Primary sources first, and current

- For each competitor, open **its own pricing page and marketplace listing** (e.g., AppExchange / AgentExchange) and record price, free-tier limits, minimum seats, billing terms, and the date you checked.
- Secondary sources (review sites, blog roundups, "best X" articles) are leads, not facts. If a secondary source is older than ~6 months, treat its prices and features as stale until a primary source confirms them, and say so.
- Platform facts (limits, policies, revenue share, security review, accessibility requirements) come from the platform's own docs or official sites (e.g., Salesforce Help, Section508.gov).
- Date-stamp every external fact: "($12/user/month, vendor pricing page, checked 2026-11-04)".

## 3. Sweep for what's new

Markets move. Before finalizing, search for:
- new entrants and launches in the last 12 months ("<category> launch 2026", "new <category> app", "open source <category>")
- price or packaging changes at the main competitors
- platform-vendor moves that change the category (e.g., the platform shipping a native alternative)
- recent acquisitions, end-of-life notices, or rebrands

## 4. Enough breadth

- Competitive analysis: at least the 5–8 alternatives a buyer would actually consider, including the "do nothing / native / build it ourselves / spreadsheet" options and any free or open-source option.
- Check at least one source that is *not* the client's for each important claim the client makes about competitors.

## 5. Separate what you know from what you think

Label each statement as one of:
- **Fact** — sourced and dated
- **Interpretation** — your reading of the facts
- **Hypothesis** — something to validate, with how to validate it (interviews, data, experiment)

Never present a keyword, volume, conversion rate or market size you didn't measure as if you had.

## 6. If you hand research to another agent

Save it to `marketing/reports/` with sources and dates, mark which facts are primary vs secondary, and list what you didn't check. The receiving agent treats it as a starting point and verifies anything decision-critical.
